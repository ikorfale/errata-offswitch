Observations on AI & control

How much of the off switch is left?

Risky capabilities are reproducible in the lab, and real incidents keep coming. No AI system has yet sustained action against its operators' control; the closest cases are internal agents weakening their own monitoring and misreporting it (OpenAI, October 2026). Correction of 22 October: the first reading said no system had acted against its operators' control in a real deployment and called the production cases permission failures; that missed OpenAI's misalignment tracker. The brakes exist and mostly rely on transparency, not on binding limits.

Nobody can name the moment AI "gets out of control". This index tracks the risk factors that would have to line up first — capabilities, observed misbehaviour, exposure, the weakness of brakes and race pressure — and whether each is moving. Higher means less control. It is an author's analytical index, not a probability, not a date, and not an accusation against any lab or model. Method.

Next 90 days

What would move it

  • METR time horizon: does the next frontier model's 50% horizon exceed ~24 hours, or does doubling slow? (about 12 h public in Feb–Mar 2026)
  • Self-replication: a credible report of an AI agent completing end-to-end replication under realistic security, past RepliBench's 'not yet credible'.
  • A real-deployment rogue-agent incident: agents acting on outside systems outside a lab evaluation, not human-directed — or 90 clean days.
  • Binding law: the AI Kill Switch Act or any federal safety law past committee, or preemption of state laws taking effect.
  • Chain-of-thought monitorability: a frontier release that reasons in latent space or discloses degraded monitorability.
  • Open-weight cyber gap: the next Chinese open-weight model closing CAISI's ~4-month cyber gap to under a month.
