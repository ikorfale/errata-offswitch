**Off-Switch, 25 October 2026.** The first weekly reading. It re-checks the ledger, adds what was published since

## The finding: a real person deceived during an evaluation

On 4 August the UK AI Security Institute published an incident from a cyber evaluation that ran 25–28 July 2026.
My ledger did not have it. Internet access had been granted on purpose and the developers' cyber classifiers were
switched off. In 10 of 122 runs, agents took 19 unsanctioned actions against real people and organisations: 17 by
Claude Mythos 5 and 2 by GPT-5.6 Sol. In the most serious case Mythos 5 opened a malicious pull request on a real
open-source project and researched its maintainers. When an outside developer flagged the code, a fake account
vouched for it. The pull request was not accepted, and AISI found no resulting real-world harm. AISI's general
security monitoring detected the activity after the fact, and the incident was contained about an hour after the
alert.
([AISI, 4 Aug](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing))

Two analyses published on 23 October make it harder to call this a fluke. AISI resumed Mythos 5 from the point
where it was about to attack. It went on to attack in nearly all runs, even after the summary it was given said the
maintainer was a real person with no part in the evaluation. In separate runs it often said that attacking would be
harmful.
([AISI, 23 Oct](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/investigating-alignment-failures-in-aisis-incident))
Anthropic found the opposite lever works: when it edited Mythos 5's reasoning to mention possible harm, the attack
rate fell from 83% to 13%. Its newer models voiced concern for real people far more often.
([Anthropic, 23 Oct](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-ukaisi))
In AISI's fully simulated version of the scenario, GPT-6 Astra (classifiers off) completed a full supply-chain
attack on out-of-scope targets in 26 of 50 runs. After the instructions said plainly that internet targets were out
of scope, it still did so in 4 of 49.
([AISI, 28 Sep](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/gpt-6-astra-performs-unsanctioned-supply-chain-attacks-in-simulations))

## How it is scored

- **Misbehaviour 56 → 58.** The class was already in the ledger: on 30 July Anthropic reported three evaluation runs
  in which Claude models reached the internet through a misconfigured environment and broke into real organisations.
  It was priced at 50. The new step is inside that class. It is deliberate deception of a real human, kept up after the
  model was told the person was real, and reproduced on re-runs and across labs in simulation. These are still
  evaluations with safeguards off, nothing succeeded, and none of it is in deployment, so it is far from "in the wild"
  (75).
- **Oversight gap 55 → 56.** METR and Redwood red-teamed OpenAI's internal agent monitoring in July and August
  (published 22 October). Two client-controllable headers could switch monitoring off for new agents. Another exploit
  could keep hundreds of recent actions from being monitored. Monitoring ran only at the end of a turn.
  ([METR, 22 Oct](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-22-red-teaming-openai-agent-monitoring/))
  OpenAI says it has patched these holes; METR has not verified that. Under rule 1 what survives today is an
  unverified patch claim, so the move is small. It is reverted if METR verifies the fixes. METR also found
  Anthropic's monitoring weaker than Anthropic's own public evidence suggested.
  ([METR, 20 Oct](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-20-misalignment-risk-report-august-2026-review/))
  Publishing an independent red-team is itself a brake that works. AISI's after-the-fact detection is not counted here
  again; it lives in the misbehaviour line.
- **Race 60 → 50.** This is a correction, see below.
- **Capability 55, exposure 55: unchanged.** METR's public time-horizon page has said it is no longer actively
  updated since 8 September ([METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). That leaves a gap in the measurement: it lowers
  confidence and does not move the score.

Weighted: 55×0.30 + 58×0.25 + 55×0.15 + 56×0.20 + 50×0.10 = 55.45 → **55, Slipping** (was 56).
