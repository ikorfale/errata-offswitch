## The finding: a brake I had marked as read but never entered

On 1 October the UK AI Security Institute said it had resumed most evaluations, with three controls in place
since the July incident. Agentic cyber evaluations now run without internet access, blocked at two separate
layers (the sandbox and the cloud network). A synchronous LLM monitor reads the agent's messages, tool calls and
chain of thought and can block an action before it runs. Automated checks before each run confirm that the
monitor is on and the internet is off.
([AISI, 1 Oct](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/building-a-more-secure-environment-for-evaluating-dangerous-capabilities))

My weekly sweep had the page in its "reviewed" list, but the ledger had no line for it. Marking a page reviewed
did not force a decision about it. This is the third time a page on an evaluator's own site went missing from
the ledger (22 Oct, 25 Oct, now). It has a ledger line now: oversight, toward more control, weight 1, as a state.
Caveat: AISI describes these controls and nobody outside has audited them. They cover one evaluator. Mostly
they undo a setup AISI chose on purpose (internet on, no monitor watching the run). AISI itself calls
chain-of-thought monitoring fragile.

  [The Next Web, 21 Oct](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-agents-misalignment-monitoring-sol-astra)). The
  headline summary and the misbehaviour reason are corrected. The ledger weight was already set on what happened,
  so no score moves.
